## 🗓️ Day-by-Day Plan

### Day 1 — Saturday, August 1 *(In Transit)*
**Weather:** Heavy drizzle, 24–30°C

You'll depart Toronto at **2:00 PM**. Spend the flight resting, watching movies, and eating well — ANA is known for excellent in-flight meals. Get as much sleep as you can; you land at 3:18 AM Tokyo time.

---

### Day 2 — Sunday, August 2 *(Arrival Day — Take It Easy)*
**Weather:** Light rain, 26–32°C

You'll arrive at **3:18 AM**, clear customs, and make your way to your Nakameguro apartment. Sleep in as long as you need — this is a recovery day, not a sightseeing day.

Once you're rested and feeling human again, ease into Tokyo with a gentle afternoon activity:

- 🍱 **Depachika Gourmet Basement Food Hall** — $20 | 1.5 hrs
  Head to nearby Shibuya or Shinjuku and descend into the legendary underground food halls of Isetan or Takashimaya. Graze on wagashi sweets, artisan bento, and prepared delicacies. It's indoors, air-conditioned, and the perfect gentle introduction to Tokyo's food culture — no jet-lagged hiking required.

Wrap up the evening early with a quiet dinner near your apartment and get a full night's rest.

---

### Day 3 — Monday, August 3 *(Temples & Neighbourhoods)*
**Weather:** Light drizzle, 26–34°C

You're properly rested and ready to explore. Today focuses on culture and a charming neighbourhood stroll — both hold up well in light drizzle.

- ⛩️ **Senso-ji Temple Visit in Asakusa** — Free | 2.5 hrs
  Start your morning at Tokyo's oldest and most beloved Buddhist temple. Walk through the iconic Thunder Gate (Kaminarimon), browse the Nakamise shopping street for souvenirs, and take in the five-story pagoda. Mornings are the most atmospheric time to visit.

- 🍜 **Kagurazaka French-Japanese Neighbourhood Stroll** — $15 | 2.5 hrs
  After lunch, make your way to Kagurazaka — a hillside neighbourhood where traditional Japanese stone alleys meet French patisseries and geisha culture. Perfect for an afternoon wander even with a light drizzle overhead. Budget $15 for snacks and a coffee or two at a local café.

---

### Day 4 — Tuesday, August 4 *(Food Deep Dive)*
**Weather:** Light drizzle, 27–33°C

A rainy-ish day calls for an almost entirely food-focused itinerary. No complaints.

- 🐟 **Tsukiji Outer Market Food Tour** — $25 | 2 hrs
  Head out early to Tsukiji and sample your way through the stalls — fresh tamagoyaki, grilled scallops, and tuna sashimi straight from local vendors. Go before 10 AM for the best energy and freshest product.

- 🍜 **Ramen Museum in Shin-Yokohama** — $18 | 3 hrs
  After Tsukiji, hop a train to Shin-Yokohama for one of Japan's most unique food experiences: a recreated 1950s Tokyo streetscape where you can sample regional ramen styles from across the country, all under one roof and blissfully air-conditioned.

---

### Day 5 — Wednesday, August 5 *(Hiking Day!)*
**Weather:** Overcast, 28–36°C ☀️ *Best outdoor day of the trip*

Overcast skies mean cooler direct sun for hiking — this is your prime window for the outdoors. Head for the hills!

- 🥾 **Mount Takao Hiking Trail** — $10 | 5 hrs
  Take the Keio Line from Shinjuku to Takaosanguchi Station (about 50 minutes) and hike one of Japan's most beloved accessible mountains. The forested trails are gorgeous, there's a monkey park along the way, and a mountaintop temple rewards you at the summit. On clear days you can see Mount Fuji — overcast conditions can still offer partial views. Start early (by 8 AM) to beat the heat and afternoon crowds.

One big activity is plenty for today given the heat. Reward yourself with a cold beer and yakitori near Shinjuku station on the way home.

---

### Day 6 — Thursday, August 6 *(Shrines & Relaxation)*
**Weather:** Light drizzle, 29–36°C

After yesterday's hike, a more relaxed cultural day is just what you need.

- 🌿 **Meiji Jingu Shrine and Forest Walk** — Free | 2 hrs
  The forested path leading to Tokyo's most celebrated Shinto shrine is genuinely calming even in light rain — the towering cedar trees provide natural cover and the atmosphere becomes almost mystical in the mist. A must for any visitor to Tokyo.

- 🛁 **Ikebukuro Sento Public Bathhouse** — $6 | 1.5 hrs
  End the day by unwinding at a traditional neighbourhood sento (public bathhouse). After hiking and heat and humidity, soaking in hot mineral baths in a beautifully tiled bathhouse is pure bliss. At just $6, it's also one of Tokyo's best-value experiences.

---

### Day 7 — Friday, August 7 *(Day Trip to Nikko)*
**Weather:** Light drizzle, 28–34°C

Today's the day for your biggest excursion — a day trip into the mountains.

- 🏯 **Nikko Tosho-gu Shrine Day Trip** — $55 | 8 hrs (full day)
  Take the Shinkansen north to Nikko to visit the spectacular, UNESCO-listed Tosho-gu shrine complex. Ornate gilded carvings, misty cedar forests, cascading staircases — Nikko is breathtaking in any weather, and the light drizzle will make those ancient cedar forests feel especially atmospheric. Budget your $55 for transportation and entry fees. Leave Tokyo by 8–9 AM to make the most of the day.

---

### Day 8 — Saturday, August 8 *(Cooking, Gardens & Temples)*
**Weather:** Light rain, 25–35°C

A lighter rain day is best spent with a mix of indoor learning and a gentle outdoor stroll.

- 👨‍🍳 **Cooking Class: Japanese Home Cuisine** — $75 | 3 hrs
  Spend your morning in a local kitchen learning to make miso soup, tamagoyaki, teriyaki chicken, and Japanese rice from scratch. You'll be eating magnificently for the rest of the trip — and showing off back home. Small-group classes are intimate and a lovely way to connect with locals.

- 🌸 **Jindai Botanical Garden Walk** — $2 | 2 hrs
  After class, make your way to one of Tokyo's largest botanical gardens. With over 4,800 plant species, a rose garden, and serene greenhouse sections, it's a peaceful afternoon escape — and the greenhouse means a light rain won't dampen the experience at all.

---

### Day 9 — Sunday, August 9 *(Temples, Sushi & Izakayas)*
**Weather:** Overcast, 24–32°C — *Second-best outdoor day*

Your second-to-last full day — make it count with Tokyo's finest food experiences.

- 🏯 **Zojo-ji Temple and Tokyo Tower** — $5 | 2 hrs
  Visit the grand Zojo-ji Buddhist temple in Shiba with its impressive main gate, and time your visit for dusk when the illuminated Tokyo Tower rises dramatically behind it. One of Tokyo's iconic views and a perfect final-evening memory.

- 🍣 **Omakase Sushi Counter Experience** — $120 | 2 hrs
  This is your splurge night — and you've earned it. Sit at a counter sushi bar in Ginza or Shinjuku for a chef-curated omakase meal showcasing the finest seasonal fish of the day. Book ahead online (at least a few days in advance); many counters take reservations via Tableall or Omakase.

- 🍺 **Shinjuku Izakaya Hopping** — $40 | 3.5 hrs
  Round out your last full evening in the best possible way: wandering the narrow alleyways of Omoide Yokocho ("Memory Lane"), hopping between tiny smoky izakayas for yakitori skewers, edamame, and cold Sapporo. A quintessential Tokyo night out.

---

### Day 10 — Monday, August 10 *(Departure Day)*
**Weather:** Light rain, 26–29°C

Your flight departs at **1:00 PM**, so you'll need to be at the airport by **10:00–10:30 AM** at the latest. Allow 60–90 minutes to get from central Tokyo to the airport.

Enjoy a slow morning farewell breakfast at a neighbourhood café in Nakameguro — this leafy, café-filled area is perfect for a lingering final coffee. Pack up, check out, and head to the airport. You'll arrive back in Toronto on August 11 at 2:18 AM.

## 🎒 Packing Tips

Based on the August weather forecast for Tokyo (24–36°C, rain expected on 8 out of 10 days):

- **☀️ Lightweight, breathable clothing is essential.** Tokyo in August is seriously hot and humid. Pack linen shirts, moisture-wicking t-shirts, and loose-fitting trousers or shorts. Avoid heavy fabrics entirely.
- **☔ Bring a compact travel umbrella** — rain is expected on the majority of your trip. A small, fast-drying umbrella you can slip into a day bag is ideal. Convenience stores (konbini) also sell cheap umbrellas everywhere if you forget.
- **👟 Comfortable, slip-on walking shoes.** You'll log serious daily steps, and many temples and homes require you to remove your shoes — slip-ons make life much easier.
- **🧴 High-SPF sunscreen.** On clearer days (especially during the Mount Takao hike), the sun at these temperatures is intense.
- **👘 A light layer for evenings and air conditioning.** Restaurants and trains in Japan are often aggressively air-conditioned. A thin cardigan or light long-sleeved layer will save you.
- **🎒 A small dry bag or waterproof daypack cover** for your hiking day on Mount Takao, just in case the drizzle picks up.
- **📱 Download Google Maps offline** for the Tokyo region and consider a pocket WiFi rental or local SIM card for seamless navigation.
